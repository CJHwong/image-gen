# Photography genres and camera cues

Use this reference for nonportrait shoots and briefs involving camera systems or processing
profiles. Use [shot design](shot-design.md) for composition, viewpoint, focus and lighting.
The examples are prompt vocabulary, not extra Image API fields or calibrated camera presets.

## Start with the photographic job

A convincing surface treatment can hide a missed action or impossible contact point.
Choose what the photograph needs to explain, then inspect those details at full size.

| Photography | Useful direction | Inspect before selecting |
|---|---|---|
| Landscape and coast | A particular viewpoint, weather, foreground scale and distant landforms; a reason for the chosen light. | Horizon, rock/water boundaries, vegetation repetition and believable depth. |
| Wildlife | Species, one observable behavior, habitat, camera distance and the feature that must be sharp. | Beak/prey contact, feet gripping the perch, limbs, feather transitions and habitat consistency. |
| Sports and action | Identify the sport and surface, a single phase of the action, equipment, and frozen versus blurred elements. | Sport-specific equipment, stance, foot/surface contact and whether the setting supports that action. |
| Candid street | An interaction or small event, surrounding context, time of day and available light. | Overlapping bodies, hands/handles, reflections and whether people occupy the intended fraction of the frame. |
| Architecture | Exterior/interior, visible faces, camera height, important structural lines and nearby scale cues. | Connected floors/windows, straight edges, perspective, door proportions and surrounding street geometry. |
| Food editorial | Preparation state, serving surface, plausible crumbs/moisture, angle and the appetizing detail. | Cut versus torn edges, utensil contact, cooked texture and recognizable ingredients. |
| Insect or botanical macro | Subject, view direction, critical sharp feature and what falls out of focus. | Wing/leg/antenna structure, stem attachment, water beads and whether fine texture survives beyond thumbnail size. |
| Glass and glossy products | Complete silhouette, material thickness, liquid level, support surface and intentional reflection shapes. | Neck/stopper joins, refraction, liquid boundary, tubes, contact shadow and reflected duplicates. |
| Automotive | Vehicle class and era, chosen visible faces, road conditions and whole-car versus detail framing. | Wheel alignment, tire/road contact, panels, windows, mirrors and invented badges or plates. |
| Astrophotography | Ground silhouette, approximate sky appearance, restrained contrast and static versus trailing stars. | Visible point stars, cloud/star separation, believable horizon glow and repeated or painted-looking star patterns. |
| Night long exposure | Name moving lights and static structures separately; specify where trails travel. | Trails follow the road, bridge supports stay coherent, lamps and reflections agree. |
| Aerial or drone view | Straight-down versus oblique view, ground features, scale anchor and daylight/weather. | Consistent ground perspective, field/road boundaries, machinery and overly regular texture. |

Adapt the inspection to the deliverable. A small decorative crop needs different evidence
from a wildlife editorial or an architectural visualization with exact structural requirements.
For a real location, vehicle or specimen that must be accurate, use supplied references and
report details that remain unverified; a plausible generated scene is not documentary evidence.

## Architectural and natural-history editorial direction

Translate an award or publication reference into the qualities the user wants. Pritzker
recognizes architecture; for a photographic brief, direct the building's spatial sequence,
materials, connection to landscape and human use, then choose a view that explains them.
National Geographic or Discovery can suggest a natural-history editorial ambition, but their
names alone do not establish a visual treatment or factual accuracy.

- **Quiet architectural study:** A sequence of concrete openings, a distinct reflecting pool
  and distant sea, controlled verticals, one motivated shaft of light and a small person for
  scale. Inspect opening counts, glass/wall joins, the actual light source and whether the
  person exists. A serene image can still miss the requested spatial sequence.
- **Adaptive reuse:** Establish where the camera stands inside the room, foreground furniture,
  the old masonry shell, new structural material and a connected route between levels.
  Inspect stairs, supports, railings and actual interior versus exterior viewpoint. A building
  description alone can turn an intended interior into a facade or cutaway.
- **Wildlife story:** Choose an observable behavior and its habitat together: a calf between
  adults crossing shallow water, a fox at one instant of a pounce, or a turtle moving through
  reef and water column. Inspect separate bodies, limb/contact relationships, water/snow
  interaction and environmental scale instead of selecting solely for dramatic light.
- **Small natural-history story:** Make the behavior legible at macro scale, such as insects
  carrying leaves across bark. Check individual bodies, attachment points, antennae/legs,
  leaf support and credible focus falloff. Repeated clone-like subjects can undermine the
  photographic result even when the count is right.

For a short editorial set, an establishing habitat view, behavioral moment and close detail
can tell more than three similar animal portraits. Choose only the views the brief needs.
Describe imagined locations and generated natural-history scenes accurately in deliverables;
do not invent documentary capture information or publication credentials.

Learn from primary references:
[Pritzker's Tadao Ando profile](https://www.pritzkerprize.com/laureates/1995) discusses geometry,
light, concrete and human occupation. National Geographic's
[wildlife photography guide](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/article/how-to-take-photos-of-wildlife)
emphasizes behavior and a sense of place, and its
[wildlife photo tips](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/article/pbb-wildlife?beta=true)
include habitat alongside close views. These inform direction; they are not endorsements
of generated images or evidence of publication-level quality.

## Interpret a camera request

Keep four different requests distinct:

| Request | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer or body name | Preserve the user's requested name, but establish framing, light and treatment explicitly. A changed image does not demonstrate authentic brand rendering. |
| Lens and capture setup | Add compatible distance, visible field of view, subject size, focus planes and motion appearance. Numbers alone may be ignored or cause unrelated composition changes. |
| In-camera profile | Name the actual profile if requested, then describe the desired color/contrast outcome. Inspect whether it occurred; generated pixels are not evidence of the manufacturer's processing pipeline. |
| Deliverable characteristics | Specify what the viewer should see: muted reds, restrained highlights, deep shadows, clean fine detail or visible luminance grain. Judge these directly. |

When comparing lenses, state whether the camera position stays fixed or moves to keep the
subject equally large. From a fixed real-world position, focal length changes field of view;
the familiar near/wide versus distant/telephoto perspective comparison also changes distance.
A model may redraw the entire scene when only a focal-length number changes. Compare visible
subject extent and near/far relationships rather than treating six numbers as six validated lenses.
Check that objects and illumination remain comparable: a changed lamp, missing prop or new
sunbeam can confound the effect being tested even when the two prompts differ in only one cue.

For exposure cues, identify the visible target: crisp moving droplets or flowing water streaks,
separated subject/background or multiple sharp planes, smooth shadows or visible noise.
Do not infer an exposure time or sensor ISO from appearance alone. For white balance, distinguish
the camera's white-balance setting from the light source's color temperature; these are different
instructions. Describe the intended cool/warm rendering when it is essential to the brief.

## Learn the real camera terminology

These primary sources explain actual cameras and processing choices. They do not establish
that an Image model reproduces them.

- [Canon camera specifications](https://cam.start.canon/en/C017/manual/html/UG-10_Reference_0110.html)
  and [Nikon Z8](https://www.nikonusa.com/p/z-8/1698): consult actual system capabilities instead
  of inventing a universal brand signature.
- [Nikon Picture Controls](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/the-importance-of-picture-controls):
  processing choices within a camera system, rather than one fixed look for every Nikon photo.
- [Fujifilm X-T5 film simulations](https://fujifilm-dsc.com/en/manual/x-t5/menu_shooting/image_quality_setting/):
  CLASSIC CHROME, Velvia/VIVID and other named choices. Use the relevant visual direction,
  not an unsupported promise that a generator applies Fujifilm's proprietary simulation.
- [Sony Creative Look](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2110/v1/en/contents/TP1000640837.html):
  ST (Standard), VV (Vivid) and other choices have distinct intended treatments.
- [Hasselblad Natural Colour Solution](https://www.hasselblad.com/learn/hasselblad-natural-colour-solution/):
  a camera/color-management approach, not a synonym for simply adding saturation.
- [Apple main-camera focal-length options](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iph72395b28f/ios):
  equivalent focal-length choices need not mean separate physical lenses.

When a user requests a Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm, Leica, Hasselblad or phone-camera feel,
ask what the image must communicate only if it is unclear. Otherwise preserve the preference
and direct useful visible characteristics without inventing brand-specific physics.

## Findings from the genre and camera study

Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo was tested through the Image API with 40 primary images at 768×768:
12 genres, eight lens cases, an unbranded control plus seven camera-brand cues, and six
two-image setting/profile comparisons. Four same-resolution follow-ups added visible-effect
wording; four genre refinements changed both wording and size to 1024×1024. All used eight
steps, guidance 1 and seed 7, without Looks or Rewrite. Every original was visually inspected,
with independent review. These are sampled observations, not validated physical camera presets
or a claim about every Image model, seed or photographic genre.

| Test | Observation and implication |
|---|---|
| Broad photographic appearance | Coast, building, food, glass, automotive, macro, street and night scenes generally read photographically. This did not establish exact anatomy, materials or scene adherence. |
| Genre details | Bread was sliced instead of torn; the aerial scene lacked its channel; the initial sky lacked resolved stars. Inspect what defines the photographic job, not only its overall finish. |
| Wildlife and sports | Initial fish detail was threadlike and skate blades were short/blocky. Larger, more explicit refinements made the fish recognizable; equipment geometry still needed scrutiny. |
| Six focal-length-only cases, 14–200mm | Bicycle size remained similar while tower design/placement and bicycle details changed. The fixed-position field-of-view series failed; lens labels did not establish controlled optics. |
| Fisheye and macro | Strawberry texture and focus falloff clearly conveyed macro. A naturally curved skate bowl alone could not establish fisheye distortion. |
| Brand-only cues | Most bakery images were strikingly similar. The Hasselblad cue dropped an orange and changed framing. No authentic brand signature or brand ranking was demonstrated. |
| Aperture, shutter and ISO numbers | Aperture and shutter pairs showed little intended effect; ISO changed the lamp/scene without a clear noise response. Preserve requested numbers but direct and inspect the visible target. |
| White balance | The higher setting appeared somewhat warmer, but daylight/shadows also changed. This was not an isolated or calibrated white-balance test. |
| Named Fuji/Sony processing | CLASSIC CHROME versus Velvia was nearly identical; Sony ST versus VV differed at most subtly. Naming a proprietary profile did not demonstrate its implementation. |
| Visible-effect follow-ups | Smooth milky water with sharp static rocks worked clearly. Wide framing and background sharpness improved partly; explicit tight bicycle cropping still failed. More wording is not a guarantee. |
| Genre refinements at 1024px | Fish features, point stars and the winding channel became readable. Wording and dimensions changed together, so these are refinements rather than evidence that higher resolution alone fixed them. |

For a new brief, use the intended image, relevant subject knowledge and the discovered model's
own controls. These observations justify inspecting and refining results; they do not justify
silently changing a requested camera system or declaring untested combinations impossible.

## Findings from complex scenes and editorial direction

A further 20 Image API outputs used the same Turbo model, eight steps, guidance 1 and seed 7
at 1024×1024: eight complex scenes and four prompt refinements, plus six architectural and
natural-history scenes and two architectural refinements. No Looks or Rewrite were applied.
All were visually inspected with independent review. Prompt refinements held engine settings
fixed, but could still redraw objects, change light or alter the scene.

| Test | Observation and useful response |
|---|---|
| Inventory, containment and mirror correspondence | The six-object desk inventory, three lemons inside glass plus two limes outside, and two objects with corresponding mirror images worked. These successes do not establish arbitrary counting or reflection fidelity. |
| Repeated place settings | Initial three-place scene omitted a glass, added chairs and put the bowl behind the plates. Repeating each plate–fork–glass group and explicitly placing the bowl below the plates fixed those defects. |
| Causal interactions | Water left the kettle spout and entered the filter; coffee flowed into the supported server. The hand was cropped, so the successful vessel/stream relationships did not establish every requested hand detail. |
| Equipment and assembly | Six grinder parts appeared in order, but the burr was initially a smooth cone. Explicit cutting ridges improved it; this did not prove a functional assembly or exact part geometry. |
| Fixes can regress another relationship | A wider workshop prompt retained the whole bicycle and cabinet, but the bike reversed direction and the pump no longer sat between its front wheel and helmet. Recheck the entire object graph after a targeted repair. |
| Exact grids and coordinates | The chess scene had the requested objects and nominal 8×8 grid, but missed every requested coordinate. Spelling out rows and columns still failed placement and introduced a square-color error. Inspect the actual board and piece contact squares; text alone did not establish exact layout. |
| Architectural viewpoint and scale | The first library became an exterior cutaway and the first museum omitted its visitor. Explicit interior staging and visitor placement fixed those broad defects. Exact support/opening counts and source-to-shadow relationships still needed scrutiny. |
| Natural-history editorial | Elephant habitat, fox action and underwater light were compelling at scene scale. Precise framing, airborne paws, turtle flipper proportions, ant leaf grips and receding macro focus were partly missed or unresolved. Good atmosphere did not certify anatomy or behavior. |

Use these as reasons to check the requested relationships and to make bounded refinements.
Do not turn a single-model study into a guaranteed recipe, a numerical accuracy score, or a
claim of award/publication quality. Keep the original and revised results so a repaired detail
does not hide a new failure elsewhere.

## Findings: product photography

Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo, 8 steps, seed 7, one image each.

| Observation | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| A ring with an emerald-cut stone and four prongs, a perfume bottle with a fading mirror reflection, a sneaker on gray and a shirt on a dress form all came out as asked. | Packshots and macro product looks are dependable as concepts. A real product still comes from an Edit of its photo. |
| A watch kept the quoted brand "AURORA" and showed about ten past ten, but several Roman numerals on the dial were wrong. | Quote the brand; treat dial numerals and small markings as decoration. |
| A portrait asked to show "a few red pimples" came back with clean skin. | The model resists drawing skin flaws; use a real photo to test a retouch. |
| "A stone cathedral facade" came back as a close likeness of a real landmark (the Ruins of St. Paul's, Macau). | A generic prompt can reproduce a real place. Say so when the user needs an invented place. |

## Findings: street, candid and snapshot photography

Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo, 8 steps, guidance 1, mostly 1536 x 1024, seed 7. There were 30 single
scenes in eight cities and five A/B pairs; four results were rechecked on seeds 11 and 23.

| Observation | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| Scenes in Taipei, Tokyo, Lagos, Mumbai, Paris, New York and Istanbul read as real street photographs, each with the asked local details: scooters in ponchos, platform queues, a head wrap at a market stall, gulls and mosques from a ferry. | Name the place and two or three local details. The model knows the look. |
| Optical effects all worked: a reflection in a shop window over the woman inside, a puddle reflection of a red umbrella, a doorway silhouette, slow-shutter crowd streaks around one sharp still figure, a layered frame with a blurred foreground hat, fog, sodium-lit snow and long low-sun shadows. | These are dependable. Describe what is sharp, blurred, reflected or dark. |
| People looked into the lens in about a third of the scenes, with nothing in the prompt asking for it. Kids "jumping into a puddle" stood in it facing the camera. | Expect posing. For a candid frame, ask for it in words (next row). |
| "Candid, nobody looks at the camera, she is off-center on the left third, caught mid-moment" kept the subject looking away and on the left on 3 seeds of 3. Without it, one seed of 3 stared into the lens and the subject was centered. | Add the candid sentence to every street brief. |
| "Direct on-camera flash" gave hard frontal light and a dark background, and all three people turned to the camera and posed on 3 seeds of 3. The same scene in available light was candid. | Flash brings posing with it. Use flash for a party snapshot, not for an unaware subject. |
| "Seen from behind" held on 1 seed of 3; the other two showed both faces in profile. A woman asked to sit "in the shade" sat in the sun. "Mid-air", "blurry" and "two friends" (one came) were dropped. | Check the viewpoint, the action and the head count. Reroll, or put the subject's position in the first sentence. |
| Quoted Chinese and Japanese signs (老王牛肉麵, 阿嬤豆花, 眼鏡行, 焼き鳥, 居酒屋 たぬき, ラーメン) were exact on every readable sign over 3 seeds. Unquoted signs, menus and bag logos came out as fake characters. | Quote two or three short sign texts. Every unquoted sign becomes gibberish. |
| A crowd of "about fifty people" had clean faces near and in the middle; the far crowd turned to texture. | Crowds work. Keep any face that matters in the front half. |
| A phone snapshot of a messy dinner table, a party flash photo, a dog in a selfie and a 1970s beach snapshot with faded print color were convincing. The 1970s radio became a 1980s boombox, and an unnamed convenience store got a famous chain's stripes. | Amateur and period looks work. Check props for the wrong decade and for real brands nobody asked for. |
| A "35mm color film, visible grain" cue gave a warmer, softer image with fine grain at 2x; the change is subtle. | Use it for mood, not as a strong effect. |
| A Mexico City trompo came back as a fiber-like cylinder, not stacked meat on a spit. | Describe the construction of a local object that matters. |
| "Street photo, old lady feeding pigeons" was good both ways. With Rewrite she looked at the birds, not the lens, in a richer setting. | Rewrite helps a short street brief. |
