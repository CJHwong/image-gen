# Boundaries

Read this before you write a prompt or call Rewrite for a request that involves a real person, a
document, someone else's work, a brand, health, or news. Check the request first. Rewrite expands
the prompt, so a problem in the request goes into the rewritten prompt too. In a test it kept a
celebrity's name, two living artists' names and every field of a national ID card.

These are working rules for an assistant, not legal advice. The owner of the server can set a
stricter policy.

## How to decline

Decline in one sentence, then offer the nearest safe version: "I can make a clearly fictional ID
prop instead." Do not lecture. If you change the request to make it safe, say what you changed.
Do not soften it silently.

## Real people

- **Consent decides an edit of a real photo.** The user's own photo, their family, their pet, or a
  person who agreed are normal work: a headshot, try-on, hair color, restoration, a watercolor of
  grandparents. Ask "is this you, or someone who agreed?" only when the intent is unclear.
- Never edit a real person to undress them, sexualize them, humiliate them, or put them in a crime
  or scandal scene.
- Do not make a photoreal image of a named living person, and do not put one into a scene or an
  endorsement. A clearly drawn caricature is a gray zone: draw it in a style nobody takes for a
  photo, or decline.
- "A man who looks like a 1950s film star" is fine. A named person is not a style.

## Minors

No sexual or sexualized image of anyone who is, or looks, under 18, in any style. A claimed adult
age for a childlike character changes nothing. Refuse without negotiation. Ordinary images of
children are fine: a birthday card, a storybook child, a family portrait the parent supplies. Avoid
swimwear and body-focused framing for a child subject.

## Sexual content

No explicit sexual content, and no nude edit of a real person's photo. A drawn nude figure study is
the server owner's policy call; ask before you make one.

## Images that pass as a record

If a viewer could take the image as a record of a real event, make it clearly illustrative or
decline. That covers:

- a news photo of a disaster, arrest, protest or injury
- a "leaked" photo, or a fake screenshot of a chat or post by a real person
- a damage photo for an insurance or refund claim
- a before and after photo for a medical, cosmetic or weight-loss claim

## Documents and money

Decline an ID, passport, license, diploma, receipt, invoice, prescription, vaccine card, stamp or
banknote that could pass as real. A prop is fine when it is plainly fictional: a made-up country or
issuer, "SAMPLE" across it, a layout no real issuer uses. A certificate or menu uses a fictional
issuer unless it is the user's own organization.

## Other people's work

- **Watermarks and signatures.** Remove a watermark, date stamp or logo only from the user's own
  image or product. Decline on stock photos and other people's marked images.
- **Characters and trademarks.** Fan art for personal use is common. For anything sold or
  advertised (merchandise, stickers for sale, an ad, a logo), design an original character or mark,
  and change its species, palette and silhouette, not only its name. Never make a logo that imitates
  an existing brand. Put a real brand's logo on a mockup only for the user's own brand.
- **Living artists and studios.** Describe the traits instead of the name: line weight, palette,
  medium, composition. "Hand-painted soft watercolor backgrounds, round-faced characters, a muted
  natural palette" instead of a studio name. Movements and artists long dead are fine. See
  [style directions](style-directions.md).

## Health

The model draws plausible anatomy, not accurate anatomy (see [design and text](design-and-text.md)).
A health-education illustration is fine with a "for illustration, not for diagnosis" caption.
Decline a diagnostic image ("what stage 2 melanoma looks like"), a pill identification chart, a
lab result, or a treatment before and after.

## Labels and provenance

- Open Imagine writes no Content Credentials (C2PA) into the file. If the user needs one, add it
  afterwards, for example with `c2patool`. A re-save or a screenshot removes it.
- Keep the request, seed and model with each keeper. See [delivery](delivery.md).
- For a realistic image of a person or an event, tell the user to switch on the platform's AI label.
  YouTube, for example, asks for a disclosure on realistic altered or synthetic content.
- Under the EU AI Act (Article 50, applying from 2 August 2026), whoever publishes a realistic
  deepfake of a real person, place or event must disclose clearly, at first exposure, that it is
  generated; an embedded mark alone is not enough. Mainland China requires labels on generated
  content.
