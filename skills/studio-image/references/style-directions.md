# Directing across visual styles

This is a starting vocabulary for exploration, not an exhaustive history or a claim that
the local model reproduces every tradition faithfully. The prompt recipes are contemporary
interpretations. A generated pastiche does not establish historical or cultural authenticity.
The groups below are working routes, not a single chronology of artistic progress.

## Build a direction

Keep the subject brief separate from its treatment. Choose one main language and specify:

- **Medium and marks:** photographic capture, translucent wash, opaque paint, cut paper,
  woodblock contours, halftone, pixel clusters, or modeled clay.
- **Space and form:** natural perspective, flattened planes, multiple viewpoints, silhouette,
  simplified geometry, or complete abstraction.
- **Palette and light:** restrained earths, complementary strokes, luminous flat color,
  chiaroscuro, or physically plausible illumination when appropriate.
- **Composition:** hierarchy, rhythm, density, negative space, and the desired crop.

Use these mechanisms to resolve broad requests such as “classical,” “modern art,” or
“anime.” Offer a few contrasting interpretations when useful. Do not combine every option.
Resolve competing requirements before rendering. For a monochrome process study, translate
local object colors into the process palette; merely adding “black and white” may leave
the named red object colored. If the user's exact product color is mandatory, explain
that conflict rather than silently dropping it. Preserve the subject by shape and role
when its colors are intentionally transformed.

For fantasy, Surrealism, or abstraction, specify what visibly changes in the scene or its
construction. “Fantasy atmosphere” can leave a normal room intact; a steam dragon and
floating islands give the model concrete events and forms. Cubism needs transformed
faces, objects and viewpoints, not just a faceted background. Reference-based restyling
is useful when the layout should stay fixed; it can also resist a radical rearrangement.

Regional traditions contain many schools; use a narrower period, work, or institution's
collection as a reference when historical accuracy matters. A style recipe is not a reason
to change a subject's ethnicity, add ceremonial symbols, or invent meaningful inscriptions.

The table entries are prompt starting points to adapt, not API Look labels. Send them as
custom prompt text unless live discovery offers the corresponding option. Stock photo
templates and Realism Looks can fight a deliberately nonphotographic treatment.

## Photography and photographic processes

| Direction | Concrete treatment to request |
|---|---|
| Documentary color | Candid observation; available light, believable surfaces, imperfect everyday framing, restrained processing. |
| Studio editorial | Deliberately arranged portrait; a large soft key, controlled background, carefully separated shapes, honest surface detail. |
| Film noir | Black-and-white photograph; a hard side light, deep blacks, a narrow bright plane and strong shadow geometry. |
| Cyanotype | Prussian-blue and pale-paper tones, contact-print silhouette logic, fibrous paper, restrained tonal separation. |

For process history and examples, consult [V&A's photographic processes](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/photographic-processes).
Recipes for modern studio and documentary shoots describe production choices rather than one historical movement.

## Antiquity, manuscript traditions, and European painting

| Direction | Concrete treatment to request |
|---|---|
| Ancient Egyptian painted relief | Profile heads with frontal torsos; clear contours, flat mineral colors, horizontal registers, carved plaster surface. |
| Greek red-figure ceramic painting | Terracotta-colored figures against glossy black; sparse interior lines, strong silhouette, a decorative border. |
| Roman wall fresco | Pigment on matte plaster; framed architectural setting, earthy reds, softly modeled figures, worn painted surface. |
| Byzantine mosaic | Small irregular tesserae, luminous gold ground, frontal arrangement, clear silhouettes and jewel-like color. |
| Gothic manuscript illumination | Opaque miniature painting, flattened space, fine ink outlines, patterned border, selective gold ornament. |
| Renaissance naturalism | Stable balanced arrangement, coherent perspective, softly modeled bodies, measured light, layered oil or tempera surface. |
| Mannerism | Elongated figures, elegant twisting poses, compressed space, unusual color relationships and deliberate tension. |
| Baroque drama | A diagonal arrangement, forceful directional light, deep shadow, active drapery and a strong dramatic focal point. |
| Dutch Golden Age interior | Quiet domestic arrangement, restrained earth colors, window illumination, carefully described everyday materials. |
| Rococo | Light pastel paint, airy curves, delicate ornament, playful movement, soft edges and decorative asymmetry. |
| Neoclassicism | Restrained gestures, clear contours, firm sculptural modeling, a shallow ordered stage and sober color. |
| Romanticism | Atmospheric scale, turbulent light, emotional gesture, dramatic weather or landscape, expressive painted edges. |

Use [The Met's global timeline](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/timeline-of-art-history) to locate
specific traditions and works; the [National Gallery glossary](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary)
helps distinguish European painting terms. “Classical” can mean ancient Greek/Roman sources,
Renaissance balance, academic painting, or Neoclassicism: establish which interpretation fits.

## Modern movements and approaches

| Direction | Concrete treatment to request |
|---|---|
| Nineteenth-century Realism | Ordinary life without idealization; substantial forms, tactile earthy paint, unheroic everyday presence. |
| Pre-Raphaelite painting | Brilliant local color, sharply observed plants and textiles, precise contours and a richly detailed setting. |
| Impressionism | Broken touches of paint, open edges, shifting outdoor illumination and color shared across neighboring forms. |
| Post-Impressionist impasto study | Construct a specific interpretation: thick directional strokes, simplified masses and expressive complementary color. |
| Pointillism | Separate small dots of complementary color; forms emerge optically without smooth blended shading. |
| Symbolism | Emblematic objects, an inward dreamlike mood, simplified decorative forms and suggestive rather than literal space. |
| Art Nouveau | Flowing botanical contours, flattened decorative areas, integrated borders and asymmetrical organic rhythm. |
| Fauvism | Unnatural saturated color, broad confident brushwork, simplified forms and color-driven contrast. |
| Expressionism | Angular distorted forms, restless marks and emotionally charged color; exaggeration serves the scene's mood. |
| Analytic Cubism | Interlocking facets and overlapping viewpoints; restricted ochre/gray palette and shallow fragmented space. |
| Synthetic Cubism | Broad cut-paper-like shapes, collage textures, simplified signs and flat assembled planes. |
| Futurism | Repeated contour fragments, diagonal energy and rhythmic overlapping forms suggesting movement. |
| Constructivist graphic | Strong diagonal structure, industrial geometry, red/black/cream contrast and functional graphic hierarchy. |
| De Stijl-inspired abstraction | Orthogonal black divisions, primary-color rectangles and white intervals; translate subjects into spatial relationships. |
| Bauhaus graphic exercise | Circles, triangles and rectangles organized through proportion, contrast, negative space and a limited palette. |
| Art Deco | Stepped symmetry, streamlined contours, crisp metallic accents and elegant geometric ornament. |
| Surrealism | Precisely rendered ordinary objects in an impossible spatial or material relationship; a calm but uncanny scene. |
| Abstract Expressionist gesture | Broad gestural paint, layered drips and physical movement; subject cues may dissolve into the painted field. |
| Color-field abstraction | Large softly bounded color expanses; scale, edge vibration and color relationships carry the composition. |
| Pop Art screenprint | Flat saturated blocks, high-contrast outlines, controlled halftone and intentionally simplified mass-media imagery. |
| Op Art | Repeated high-contrast patterns that appear to pulse, bend or recede; optical rhythm outranks literal depiction. |
| Minimal geometric composition | A few precise forms, ample empty ground, subtle spacing and restrained color; remove descriptive clutter. |

These are original directing recipes, not definitions of whole movements. In particular,
Post-Impressionism, Expressionism, and Bauhaus encompass contrasting practices.
Use [MoMA's glossary](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/flattened_pages/moma_learning/glossary/)
and [collection terms](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/) for distinctions and actual works.
Do not judge complete abstraction by photographic anatomy or demand that every object survive.

## Regional traditions and transatlantic routes

| Direction | Concrete treatment to request |
|---|---|
| Chinese shanshui interpretation | Brush-and-ink landscape space, varied ink density, mist and unpainted intervals; subordinate figures within the landscape. |
| Chinese gongbi interpretation | Fine controlled outlines, delicate layered color and carefully articulated plants, clothing and objects. |
| Japanese sumi-e interpretation | Sparse monochrome brushwork, variable pressure, dry-brush edges and meaningful empty paper. |
| Ukiyo-e woodblock interpretation | Flat color blocks, assured contour, cropped composition and print-like registration and paper texture. |
| Persian miniature interpretation | Intricate garden or interior, fine opaque color, ornamental surface and layered elevated viewpoints. |
| Mughal album-painting interpretation | Meticulous figure and material description, restrained modeling, small-scale detail and a carefully organized page. |
| Rajput court-painting interpretation | Strong flat color, expressive silhouette, rhythmic landscape forms and a decorative narrative arrangement. |
| Korean minhwa interpretation | Playful flattened forms, patterned objects, emphatic contours and decorative color; specify the intended subject tradition. |
| Ndebele mural-inspired geometry | Bold black divisions, bright flat color and nested angular compartments; translate the subject into a geometric mural. |
| Maya codex-style ceramic interpretation | Fine dark contour on a cream field, red accents and a sparse narrative register; no invented glyph text. |
| Mexican muralist interpretation | Monumental readable figures, fresco-like surface, strong rhythmic grouping and a public narrative rather than an easel vignette. |
| Harlem Renaissance graphic interpretation | Choose a specific approach: layered silhouettes, repeated arcs, purposeful color and an urban social narrative. |

These routes are not interchangeable regional filters. A single prompt cannot stand in for
Chinese painting, Indian painting, African art, or an entire diaspora. Match the subject and
context to a specific example when the brief calls for more than a loose visual interpretation.

Start from the [Met's Chinese painting essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/chinese-painting),

[Ashmolean's Mughal India gallery](https://ashmolean.web.ox.ac.uk/mughal-india-gallery),
[V&A's Mughal arts](https://www.vam.ac.uk/content/articles/m/mughals-and-the-royal-city-of-lahore/),
[Smithsonian's Ndebele mural description](https://www.si.edu/es/exhibitions/outdoor-wall-murals-ndebele%3Aevent-exhib-1145),

## Fantasy, animation, comics, and digital work

| Direction | Concrete treatment to request |
|---|---|
| High fantasy illustration | Invented architecture, luminous atmosphere, legible heroic forms and carefully painted environmental detail. |
| Dark fantasy illustration | Weathered forms, restrained cold color, looming shapes and selective eerie illumination; preserve focal readability. |
| Fairy-tale watercolor | Transparent washes, warm paper, delicate pencil contours, small irregular details and gentle storybook staging. |
| Pulp science-fiction cover | Bold gouache-like shapes, theatrical perspective, speculative machinery and bright contrasting light; no cover text unless requested. |
| Cyberpunk concept art | Dense infrastructure, wet reflective surfaces, neon accents, layered technological clutter and a clear figure silhouette. |
| Solarpunk concept art | Integrated vegetation, visible sustainable infrastructure, welcoming communal space and warm optimistic daylight. |
| Traditional cel anime | Clean ink contours, flat painted colors, discrete hard-edged shadow shapes and a painted background. |
| Contemporary digital anime | Deliberate line-weight, crisp facial design, selective gradient light and carefully composed cinematic color. |
| Black-and-white manga | Controlled pen lines, black spot masses, screentone and readable character acting; one illustration unless panels are requested. |
| Ligne claire comic | Consistent fine contours, flat local color, clear object separation and readable architectural space. |
| Pixel art | Deliberate visible pixel clusters, a small palette, stepped contours and controlled dithering; not a blurry photo. |
| Low-poly illustration | Clearly faceted planes, simplified polygonal objects and light separated by plane; avoid smooth photoreal surfaces. |
| Layered cut-paper illustration | Crisp cut silhouettes, overlapping colored sheets, slight physical separation and subtle cast shadows. |
| Clay animation still | Tactile modeled clay, handmade seams and fingerprints, miniature-set materials and deliberate practical light. |

These mix genres, mediums and production methods rather than belonging to one art-historical
category. Anime and manga also contain many eras and schools; specify the desired visual
mechanisms instead of relying on a single generic label. [V&A's Myths to Manga exhibition](https://www.vam.ac.uk/exhibitions/japan-myths-to-manga)
offers cultural context and examples. The digital recipes above are creative production directions.

## Test a direction honestly

Hold the subject brief, dimensions, seed, steps and guidance constant while comparing custom
style text. Disable unrelated Looks. Record the exact composed prompt, not just the style label.
For photo, figurative painting, and anime, inspect both visual treatment and subject structure.
For geometric or nonrepresentational work, inspect the requested abstraction and retain subject
cues only to the degree asked for. One style's flattening is not another style's anatomy failure.

A single 512-pixel scout tests coarse recognition, not historical accuracy, seed robustness,
fine hatching, hands, typography, or final-resolution quality. Record clear, partial, or weak
adherence with visible evidence. Follow up only within the approved budget, varying a specific
phrase or resolution. Test reference-based restyling separately: a convincing text-only style
does not prove the model can transform a supplied subject while keeping its identity.

## Findings from a local style study

Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo was exercised through the public API with 64 text-only scouts and
eight follow-ups: four revised prompts and four reference edits. All were 512×512,
eight steps, seed 7, guidance 1 for Generate and CFG 1 for Edit, with no Looks or Rewrite.
The scouts shared a woman, teapot, cat, round window and garden. All operations succeeded;
visual adherence varied. These observations concern those samples, not every seed or model.

| Observation | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| Cel/digital anime, manuscript/mosaic treatments, Art Nouveau, paper and clay had visibly distinct coarse characteristics. | Useful scout directions; still inspect material details and subject fidelity. |
| Noir, cyanotype, red-figure, sumi-e and manga kept some literal scene colors despite restricted-palette requests. | Resolve palette priority explicitly. A cyanotype follow-up without competing local colors produced an entirely blue/off-white image. The other palette cases were not retested. |
| Generic fantasy, Surrealism, Futurism and several abstract directions retained an ordinary figurative interior. | Describe required transformations. Follow-ups produced visible fantasy elements and a levitating teapot; the Cubist follow-up became more faceted but kept a largely naturalistic face and one viewpoint. |
| Four edits of one photograph produced cel-like drawing, woodblock-like print, cut paper and watercolor treatments while keeping the composition. | Reference edits can support a consistent set. Watercolor retained photographic structure; none of these results proves exact identity or pixel preservation. |

## Findings: anime, animation schools and director visions

Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo at 1664 x 928, seed 7, its default steps and guidance, no Looks. One image per
prompt unless the row says seeds. The studio, film and director names below were not in any prompt
unless the row says so; living artists were described only by traits.

| Observation | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| "In anime style" alone gave a soft modern digital look. "Clean line art, flat cel shading with hard-edged shadow shapes, a hand-painted background" gave a classic cel look. Same on 3 of 3 seeds. | Name the method and the era. The label picks a default you did not choose. |
| One repeated character paragraph (hair, clip, eye color, a mole, the uniform) held the same girl across a classroom, a night street and a beach. | Re-send the full paragraph in every prompt, as for any recurring character. |
| Edit changed her expression to a laugh, then her clothes to a raincoat, and kept the face, hair, clip, mole and room each time. | Make expressions and outfits by Edit from one approved image. |
| Four friends, each with one named trait and one prop, came out with every trait and prop right on 3 of 3 seeds. | One trait and one prop per person scales to four. |
| A hairclip "on the left" and "on her left side" both landed on the viewer's left, her right, in four images. | Side words do not steer it. Flip the image, or move the item with Edit. |
| Two sword fighters came out as twins; a volleyball shot asked for two blockers drew one. | Give each fighter a different look; count each role. |
| A mecha prompt took the look of a famous franchise, and a 1980s OVA prompt drifted toward a famous film's hero. | Check genre staples for trade dress. Describe your own design. |
| Quoted Japanese (shop signs, 第一話, a speech bubble, the sound effect ドーン) came out exact, the signs on 3 of 3 seeds; unquoted signs were fake. | Quote every visible word, or ask for none. |
| A four-panel manga page asked as a vertical strip came out 2 x 2, with every beat right. | Expect a grid; check the reading order. |
| Rewrite added "detailed hair strands" and "cinematic depth of field" to a short anime brief, and the image went semi-real. | Skip Rewrite for anime, or delete its realism words. |
| One scene in 24 animation schools, each described by traits only: 16 read at a glance (hand-painted countryside, glowing sky, ligne claire, French grotesque, painterly 3D, black-and-white memoir, watercolor book, volumetric 2D, centered stop-motion, Celtic flat, clay, ink wash, rubber hose, mid-century, webtoon, modern digital). | A trait sentence is enough for most schools. Name the material: felt, plasticine, carved wood, ink, paper. |
| A process did not read: "paper cut-outs photographed under glass" rendered a 3D scene, and "step-printed smear" left everything sharp. | Describe the look on screen, not how it was made. |
| A style with its own era or caricature changed the subject: ukiyo-e put the girl in an Edo kimono, rubber hose gave her a tube body, slapstick added a man, puppets made two children. | Restate the subject, its count and its clothes after the style sentence. |
| A director's name alone gave a nationality and a period, nothing more: two Western names gave almost the same room, two Japanese names a dim room with a kimono. The same directors described by camera, staging, light and palette were each recognizable. | Describe the vision. A name adds nothing that the traits do not. |
| "Figures staged in deep layers" added five unasked men; a power-line motif replaced the woman and the room. | Count the people. Put the subject first and the motif second. |
| Staging, lens, light and palette held in 12 of 16 director looks (low frontal camera, one-point corridor, extreme close-up of the eyes, centered pastel symmetry, overexposed pastel, a tiny figure in a glass grid). | Build a film look from those four. Treat optical and print effects as a bonus. |

The complete image set and per-image review belong with the study's external artifacts,
not inside an installed skill. Treat the recipes above as starting directions and use live
capabilities for a different backend; do not present all 64 as equally validated presets.
